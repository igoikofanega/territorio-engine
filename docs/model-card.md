# Model card — `vaciamiento`

Predicts the **percentage change in a Spanish municipality's population over 5 years**.

Registered in MLflow as `vaciamiento`. Numbers here come from
[`docs/evaluacion/informe.md`](evaluacion/informe.md), regenerated by `make evaluar`.
Last updated: **2026-09-06**.

---

## Intended use

| | |
|---|---|
| **Task** | Regression. Target = `(pop[T+5] / pop[T] − 1) × 100` |
| **Unit** | One Spanish municipality (`cod_municipio`, 5-digit text) in one base year |
| **Scope** | 8,131 municipalities with population data, 2015→ |
| **Users** | Public-interest analysis, journalism, local planning, civic tech |
| **Outputs** | Point estimate + q10/q90 uncertainty band + two "driver" labels |

**Intended**: comparing municipalities, spotting trends worth investigating, seeing which
towns are beating or missing their own trajectory, and as one input among several for
territorial analysis.

## Out of scope — do not use this for

- **Deciding anything about an individual municipality on its own.** For a village of 150
  people the uncertainty band is wider than most decisions would tolerate. See the error
  by size below.
- **Causal claims or policy attribution.** The "drivers" are a ranking heuristic
  (importance × correlation sign × deviation from median). Nothing here identifies a
  causal effect, so "the town is shrinking *because* rent is high" is not supported.
- **Anything beyond 5 years.** The honest answer past that horizon is that nobody knows.
- **Individuals.** The model has no personal data and says nothing about any person.
- **Reading the risk probability as a literal frequency.** See calibration below.

## Model

`HistGradientBoostingRegressor` — `max_iter=400`, `learning_rate=0.05`,
`max_leaf_nodes=31`, `l2_regularization=1.0`, `random_state=0`. No hyperparameter search:
the goal of this project is measuring the model well, not squeezing the last decimal.
Missing values are handled natively, which matters because the matrix is genuinely sparse.

Two extra quantile models (q10, q90) produce the uncertainty band.

## Data

17 features from 15 public sources, on the `municipality × year` key: demography (log
population, density, ageing, previous 3-year growth), economy (registered unemployment per
1,000, net income), housing (reference rent), climate (temperature, rainfall, clear days,
minimum temperature), connectivity (fibre coverage), isolation (km to health services, km
to the provincial capital), migration (% foreign-born), and provincial vital rates.

Full source list and licences: [`NOTICE`](../NOTICE).

### Known data limitations that affect the model

- **Vital rates are provincial, not municipal.** INE does not publish births and deaths per
  municipality in this series. Applying provincial rates to municipal populations is an
  **estimate, not a measurement**, and the approximation hurts most in small towns — which
  are the object of the project.
- **Fibre coverage has no history.** SETELECO publishes a snapshot of the current rollout,
  with no year column, so the same value is applied to every base year. This *is* a leakage
  risk — fibre arrived earlier in municipalities that were already growing — and it cannot
  be lagged with the available data. Removing it costs 0.06 pp of MAE; it is kept with the
  limitation declared. If SETELECO ever publishes a series, it must go through the same
  as-of rule as the other yearly features.
- **Climate is a climatic normal**, not a yearly series. A 30-year normal does not encode
  the population change of 2015-2020, so applying it to all base years is legitimate.
- **No municipal lineage (SCD2).** Mergers and splits break series continuity silently.
- **No data-quality flags yet.** Statistical secrecy, imputation and masking are not
  flagged per cell. This is the project's own most-violated principle and is tracked as
  open debt.

## Evaluation

Rolling-origin backtest, one fold per validation base year, always training on the past
only. Base years derive from real data coverage, never `max(year)`.

| Model | MAE (pp) | R² |
|---|---|---|
| Persistence (assume no change) | 7.65 | — |
| Trend (extrapolate recent growth) | 10.02 | — |
| **HistGradientBoosting** | **5.74 ± 0.28** | **0.36** |

**Error by municipality size** — the number that matters most here:

| Size | n | MAE | If nothing changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| <500 | 4,001 | 8.53 | 9.83 |
| 500-2,000 | 1,871 | 4.24 | 5.88 |
| 2,000-10,000 | 1,500 | 3.04 | 5.47 |
| >10,000 | 759 | 2.33 | 4.63 |

**Residual spatial autocorrelation**: Moran's I = 0.104 (p = 0.001, k = 8). The error is
not randomly distributed on the map; neighbouring municipalities fail in the same
direction. There is regional structure the features do not capture.

**Worst provinces** (INE code): 19 Guadalajara (MAE 15.02), 42 Soria (11.40), 16 Cuenca
(9.77), 26 La Rioja (9.61), 40 Segovia (9.56) — the emptying interior, where the model is
least reliable and where it is most likely to be consulted.

### Known evaluation limitation

A **full temporal embargo is not feasible**. The target looks 5 years ahead, and population
data starts in 2015, so contiguous folds share trajectory across the train/validation
boundary. With a 2-year embargo the MAE rises to 6.16 — that gap is the price of the
overlap, and the reported 5.74 should be read as mildly optimistic.

## Companion classifier — depopulation risk

Will this municipality lose more than 10% of its population in 5 years?
`HistGradientBoostingClassifier` + isotonic calibration, evaluated on a year the calibrator
never saw.

| | |
|---|---|
| AUC | 0.844 |
| Brier, calibrated | 0.0760 |
| Brier, uncalibrated | 0.0753 |
| Event base rate | 0.098 |

**Calibration does not help out of sample.** Above 40% the model promises more than
happens, and the high-probability bins hold too few municipalities for isotonic regression
to correct. The traffic light **ranks well but does not quantify well**: use the
green/amber/red levels, not the percentage.

## Ethical considerations

The model describes decline in places where people live. A prediction of emptying, shown
without its uncertainty, can become self-fulfilling — it can discourage investment in
exactly the towns that most need it. That is why the interface always shows the band, why
the risk output is a level rather than a number, and why the "drivers" are labelled as
correlational.
